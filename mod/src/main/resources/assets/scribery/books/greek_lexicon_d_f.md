## Page 1 — A Few Words Before the Words {#preface}

You're back. And you brought emeralds. Lovely.

I did mention there would be more letters. Here they are: delta, epsilon and zeta.

Now, the title says D to F. Greek, however, has no letter F. It never did. But "D–F" looked far tidier on the cover, and I decided not to argue with the cover.

Be warned: epsilon is enormous. Well over a hundred words begin with it. I don't know why. Ask the Greeks.

Zeta, on the other hand, has barely eight. The Greeks seem to have run out of enthusiasm somewhere around the sixth letter.

All 184 words here appear in the Gospel of John, and the entries work exactly as in the first book: the word as a dictionary lists it, its sounds in Latin letters, what kind of word it is, and what it means.

If something looks oddly familiar, that's because half your English vocabulary comes from Greek. You're welcome.

Carry on.

— John Koine
